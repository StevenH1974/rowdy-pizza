Dark Rye Blend Dough: The Crust That Makes a Reuben a Pizza

There are pizza doughs that serve as a neutral canvas for toppings. And then there are pizza doughs that are part of the flavor story, doughs that contribute something so specific and so intentional that the toppings would be incomplete without them.

The dark rye blend dough is the second kind.

Built from a 70/30 blend of bread flour and dark rye flour with caraway seeds worked directly into the dough, this crust tastes like the bread a great Reuben sandwich is made on. That's not an accident. It's the entire point. When you bite into a slice of Reuben pizza on this crust, the experience is complete in a way that a standard pizza dough could never achieve. The corned beef, the sauerkraut, the Swiss, the thousand island: they all make sense on this crust because the crust is speaking the same flavor language.

This is a Rowdy Dough. Bold and specific.

Here's how to make it.

Why the 70/30 Blend

Dark rye flour has an intensely earthy, distinctive flavor but almost no gluten-forming protein. Used alone it produces a dense, crumbly dough that tears rather than stretches and bakes up heavy rather than light. The 70/30 blend, 315g of bread flour to 135g of dark rye, is the sweet spot that delivers authentic rye character without sacrificing the workability and structure that pizza dough requires.

The bread flour contributes the protein and gluten strength that holds everything together and produces a crust with proper chew and structure. The dark rye contributes the earthy flavor, the darker color, and that distinctive quality that makes every bite taste like it belongs under a Reuben.

The caraway seeds are the finishing touch that takes the blend from rye-flavored to genuinely rye bread in character. Added directly to the dry ingredients and given 72–96 hours of cold fermentation to infuse throughout the dough, the caraway flavor distributes into every strand of gluten in a way that short fermentation simply cannot replicate. By the time this dough comes out of the refrigerator it smells like a rye bread bakery and bakes up tasting like one.

Ingredient Notes

Bread Flour

The structural foundation. 315g provides enough protein and gluten strength to hold the rye content together and produce a crust that stretches cooperatively and bakes up with proper chew.

Dark Rye Flour

The character ingredient. Dark rye is more intensely flavored than light rye, with a deeper, earthier quality that is exactly what this dough needs. Do not substitute light rye. The flavor difference is significant and the dark rye is what makes this crust distinctly Reuben.

Caraway Seeds

The detail that makes the difference. 1 tsp worked into the dry ingredients before any liquid is added distributes the seeds evenly throughout the dough. The cold ferment extracts and distributes the caraway flavor into every bite of the finished crust. In a same-day dough, caraway seeds taste like seeds sitting in bread. In a 72-hour cold ferment dough, they taste like the whole crust is rye bread.

Instant Yeast

Same amount as the NY style dough: 4g. Less yeast means slower fermentation and more flavor development. The cold ferment does the work.

Salt, Sugar, Olive Oil

Same supporting roles as every other dough on The Rowdy Pizza. Salt strengthens the gluten and controls fermentation. Sugar feeds the yeast. Olive oil adds tenderness and flavor.

Warm Water

Back to warm water at 105°F rather than room temperature. Unlike the NY style and Neapolitan cold ferment doughs this recipe uses warm water because the rye content benefits from a slightly more active yeast start before the cold ferment slows everything down.

Process Notes

The Knead

This dough will feel slightly different from pure bread flour dough: darker in color, slightly denser, with a subtle earthy smell from the rye flour. That's all correct and expected. Knead for 8–10 minutes until smooth with the slow spring back finger test. Don't over-knead. Dark rye flour has almost no gluten-forming protein and doesn't benefit from extended kneading the way bread flour does. 8–10 minutes is the limit.

The Cold Ferment

Divide immediately after kneading. Shape into tight balls, coat generously in olive oil, place in individual airtight containers or tightly wrapped plastic wrap, and refrigerate straight away. Mixing on Wednesday night and refrigerating means you have a 72–96 hour cold ferment by Saturday or Sunday, which is ideal for this dough. The caraway flavor needs that time.

The 2-Hour Rest

Same as the NY style dough. Pull both balls 2 hours before cooking. Dark rye blend dough is slightly less extensible than pure bread flour dough because of the rye content, so the full 2 hours at room temperature is especially important. Don't rush it.

Stretching

Use the knuckle stretch technique with patience. The rye content makes this dough slightly more resistant to stretching than pure bread flour. Rest and return whenever it resists. Aim for a 10–11 inch round rather than the thinner NY style stretch. This dough wants to be slightly thicker: the hearty Reuben toppings need a crust with substance underneath them.

On the Steel

Preheat to 500°F for a minimum of 1 hour. The dark rye flour gives this crust a naturally darker color than bread flour dough. Don't mistake the darker color for burning. Look at the edges and the bottom for doneness cues. Bake for 7–9 minutes at 500°F.

Equipment Notes

Kitchen Scale

The blend ratio matters. 315g bread flour and 135g dark rye measured accurately produces a consistent result. Volume measuring this particular recipe introduces enough variation to affect the flavor and texture balance between the two flours.

Individual Airtight Containers

Same requirement as every cold ferment dough on The Rowdy Pizza. Each ball in its own sealed container with no air exposure during the 72–96 hour ferment.

Pizza Steel and Peel

Same requirement as every cold ferment dough on The Rowdy Pizza. Each ball in its own sealed container with no air exposure during the 72–96 hour ferment.

At a Glance

  • Makes: two 10-11 inch pizzas
  • Active time: 25 minutes
  • Cold ferment: 72-96 hours

The Recipe

Ingredient Weight Volume
Bread flour 315g 2½ cups
Dark rye flour 135g 1 cup + 2 tbsp
Warm water (105°F) 290g 1¼ cups
Instant Yeast 4g 1¼ tsp
Salt 9g 1½ tsp
Sugar 8g 2 tsp
Olive Oil 14g 1 tbsp
Caraway seeds 5g 1 tsp
Olive oil for containers As needed As needed
Cornmeal for peel As needed As needed

Instructions

Prep Night

  1. Weigh and measure. Weigh all ingredients separately. The blend ratio is important: accuracy here determines the flavor and texture balance of the finished crust.
  2. Mix dry ingredients. Whisk bread flour, dark rye flour, instant yeast, sugar, salt, and caraway seeds together until evenly distributed. The mixed flour will be noticeably darker than pure bread flour, slightly gray-brown with visible caraway seeds throughout. That color is correct and exactly what you want.
  3. Add wet ingredients. Add olive oil to warm water. Pour into dry ingredients and mix until a shaggy dough forms. The dough will look darker and feel slightly denser than bread flour dough from the very first mix. This is correct.
  4. Knead. Turn out onto a clean surface. Knead for 8–10 minutes until smooth and cohesive with a slow spring back when poked. The dough will be slightly tacky and darker in color than any dough you've made before. Do not over-knead.
  5. Divide. Immediately divide into 2 equal portions of approximately 385g each.
  6. Shape and refrigerate. Shape each portion into a tight, smooth ball. Coat generously in olive oil. Place each in its own airtight container or wrap tightly in plastic wrap. Refrigerate immediately. Label with date and time.

Pizza day

  1. Pull from refrigerator. Remove both dough balls 2 hours before cooking. Leave covered at room temperature. The rye content makes this dough particularly sensitive to cold: 2 hours is the minimum.
  2. Preheat steel. Place steel on middle rack for gas oven, highest rack for electric. Preheat to 500°F for a minimum of 1 hour. Start simultaneously with pulling the dough.
  3. Prep toppings. Have all toppings staged and ready before the dough touches the peel. For Reuben pizzas specifically, press sauerkraut dry in a clean towel multiple times before the dough goes on the peel. Wet sauerkraut is the number one reason Reuben pizzas disappoint.
  4. Stretch. Use the knuckle stretch technique with patience. Aim for 10–11 inches, slightly thicker than NY style. Rest and return if it resists.
  5. Top and launch. Cornmeal on peel, toppings staged, 2–3 minute window. Launch confidently.
  6. Bake. Bake for 7–9 minutes at 500°F. The darker crust color makes visual doneness harder to judge. Rely on the bottom color and edge char. Lift an edge with a spatula at 7 minutes: golden and firm means done.
  7. Rest and cut. Slide immediately off steel onto a cutting board. Rest 5 minutes before cutting.

Storage

Unbaked Dough

Refrigerate for up to 5 days from the night it was made. Freezes well after the cold ferment is complete: coat in olive oil, wrap tightly, freeze for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator, then rest at room temperature for 2 hours before stretching.

Baked Pizza

Refrigerate in an airtight container for up to 3 days. Reheat in a skillet over medium heat, covered, for 3–4 minutes. The rye crust reheats beautifully, possibly better than any other crust on The Rowdy Pizza given its dense structure.
